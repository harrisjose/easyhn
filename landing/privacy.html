Privacy

Last updated 2026-08-11.

easyhn has no server. There is nothing to send your data to, so it doesn't collect any — no analytics, no tracking, no ads, no account. This page exists only because the Chrome Web Store and Firefox Add-ons require a privacy policy from every extension; it says, in full, what easyhn does and doesn't do with what it can see.

What easyhn can see

easyhn runs only on news.ycombinator.com. It reads the HTML that page already sent your browser and replaces it with its own rendering. It never talks to any other site, and no page you read is transmitted anywhere — not to Hacker News beyond what your browser would send it anyway, and not to anyone else.

Signing in, voting, and replying

easyhn doesn't have its own login. When you sign in, you're using Hacker News's own form, and Hacker News sets the session cookie the same way it always has — easyhn never sees your password. Voting and replying work by submitting Hacker News's own links and forms with that session, exactly as the native page would. easyhn only reads the session Hacker News reports back; it never creates or stores one itself.

What's stored on your device

easyhn uses the browser's built-in storage permission to remember two things, locally:

  • Your display settings — theme, font, size, density — so they carry across your open tabs.
  • Which threads you've opened, so it can mark comments posted since your last visit. You can turn this off in Settings, which also clears what's already stored.

If your browser syncs extension storage across your own devices (Chrome and Firefox both offer this), that sync is your browser's, not easyhn's — the same mechanism any extension's settings use. Nothing here ever leaves your browser through easyhn itself.

Third parties

None. easyhn makes no network requests of its own — every request you see it make is one you could make by clicking around Hacker News directly.
